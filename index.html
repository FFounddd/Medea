I told myself it was the last time I would stand against Eter. One more cut. One more refusal. No more altars made out of people.
We were at the edge of the engine that should not have been built. A chorus of crystal, too bright to be called life, and the city that would become the Mournland waited to be unmade.
I set the sigils. I counted the seconds between each hum. I thought I could take all the damage into me and end it clean.
Medea ran. She did not ask. She did not negotiate. She took my wrist like the world was a rope and she would not let me fall through.
We thought that was it. Two names that would go silent together. She said, if you go, I go, and I believed her because she had never lied.
The light came up like a scream and the ground forgot how to be ground. I held the cut as long as the lattice would let me. I remember the taste of copper and roses. I remember Medea saying my name without any titles left.
And then the world was a bowl of glass turned inside out. The map tore. The engine tore. We did not.
I lived. Gods, I wish I had not. The price for that survival was to hear the hum keep her name without an answer.
I would do anything to see her again, even if it meant looking for her inside the crystal lattice, even if it meant listening to the wrong voices for the right echo.
